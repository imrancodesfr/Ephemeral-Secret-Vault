Cryptographic Lifecycle

A structured, end-to-end security pipeline from client-side ingestion to zero-custody distribution and one-time ephemeral recovery.

Phase I · Secret Splitting & Distributed Custody
Step 01

Client-Side Encryption

Your raw secret (seed phrase, private key, password) is encrypted via AES-256-GCM. Plaintext is never written to disk or database.

Step 02

Shamir (k-of-n) Splitting

The ciphertext key is mathematically partitioned into n distinct polynomial coordinates (x, y) over a finite Galois field.

Step 03

Zero-Knowledge Custody

The server stores only SHA-256 hash commitments. Shards are delivered to guardians privately and wiped from server memory.

Step 04

PoW Blockchain Notary

Vault creation, threshold configuration, and renewal heartbeats are mined into an immutable Proof-of-Work block with Merkle verification.

Phase II · Automated Recovery & Ephemeral Burn
Step 05

Dead Man's Switch

The owner must periodically trigger "Renew" (heartbeat). If unresponsive past expiry, the vault automatically enters RECOVERY_MODE.

Step 06

Threshold Verification

Guardians submit their shards. Once k valid shares matching on-chain commitments arrive, Lagrange polynomial interpolation reconstructs the secret.

Step 07

Ephemeral Destruction

The secret is revealed to the authorized recipient exactly once. The ciphertext, wrapped keys, and share files are permanently incinerated.

Entities & Roles

Strict separation of concerns ensures that no single entity — not even the server — possesses enough authority to compromise your vault.

Vault Owner
The Sovereign

Creator of the vault and original holder of the secret.

  • Threshold Configuration: Sets total guardians (N) and recovery threshold (K).
  • Dead Man's Switch: Defines the time-to-live deadline interval for the vault.
  • Heartbeat Renewals: Sends periodic “Renew” heartbeats to keep the vault active.
  • Emergency Action: Can trigger immediate recovery if an emergency occurs.
  • Zero Self-Custody: Cannot hold a guardian share, eliminating centralized risk.
Guardian
The Key Trustee

A trusted contact, advisor, family member, or independent device.

  • Unique Math Shard: Holds exactly 1 Shamir polynomial coordinate (x, y).
  • Zero Knowledge: A single shard reveals 0% of the underlying secret on its own.
  • Expiry Activation: Submits their shard only when notified of vault expiration.
  • ZK Hash Verification: Server validates each shard against recorded SHA-256 commitments.
Recovery Recipient
The Beneficiary

The designated heir or backup account authorized to receive the secret.

  • Designated Heir: Explicitly assigned by the owner during initial vault creation.
  • Trigger Recovery: Authorized to initiate recovery once the Dead Man's Switch expires.
  • Live Tracking: Monitors guardian shard submissions in real-time on the recovery dashboard.
  • Sole Beneficiary: The only entity who receives the decrypted secret before permanent purge.

How Cryptographic Keys Work

A breakdown of the cryptographic constructs: Master Cipher, Shamir Shards, and Zero-Knowledge Commitments.

1. Master Symmetric Cipher (AES-256-GCM)

When you input your secret, an authenticated 256-bit AES key is generated in memory. The plaintext is encrypted with an initialization vector (IV) and authentication tag. Without the exact key, brute-forcing is computationally infeasible.

IV (12 bytes) + Ciphertext + AuthTag (16 bytes)

2. Guardian Share Keys (x, y)

The AES key is treated as the secret term $a_0$ in a polynomial:
f(x) = a_0 + a_1·x + ... + a_{k-1}·x^{k-1} \pmod p
Each guardian holds point $(x_i, y_i)$. Any $k-1$ points reveal zero information about the secret, but any $k$ points reconstruct it perfectly.

{"x": 1, "y": [142, 95, 201, 88, ...]}

3. Zero-Knowledge Hash Commitments

To verify guardian submissions without storing shares on the server, the server computes:
Commitment = SHA-256(JSON.stringify(share))
Only this one-way hash is kept. When a guardian submits, their share is hashed and compared to this immutable commitment.

64-char hexadecimal SHA-256 digest

4. Ephemeral Destruction

Traditional password vaults retain encrypted blobs indefinitely. Ephemeral Vault does not. The moment recovery completes, ciphertext, wrapped keys, and share artifacts are wiped from storage, guaranteeing forward secrecy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Key technical details regarding security, threshold mechanics, and data protection.

Can the server decrypt my secret?

No. Plaintext is encrypted via AES-256-GCM before splitting. The server only holds ciphertext and one-way SHA-256 commitments. Without the threshold of guardian shares, decryption is mathematically impossible.

Can one compromised guardian steal my secret?

No. Shamir Secret Sharing guarantees information-theoretic security: holding 1 share (or any number below k) reveals mathematically zero information about the secret.

What happens if the owner does not renew?

The Dead Man's Switch triggers. When the expiry timestamp passes, the background Expiry Monitor moves the vault to RECOVERY_MODE and alerts guardians and the recovery recipient.

What if one guardian is offline or loses their share?

Because the threshold is k-of-n (e.g. 2-of-3), n - k guardians can be unavailable, and the remaining guardians can still successfully recover the secret.
